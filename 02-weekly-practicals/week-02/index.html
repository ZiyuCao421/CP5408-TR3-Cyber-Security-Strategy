Risk → Real-World Precedent

Each card links a DJI risk to a documented incident
R1 15 · High
GPS spoofing causes drone to crash in populated area
Precedent Iran captured a US RQ-170 Sentinel drone (Dec 2011) by spoofing its GPS coordinates while it landed in Iranian territory intact. Iran also briefly captured a US ScanEagle drone (2012) by spoofing.
Sector Military UAVs — same navigation stack as commercial drones
Source: US Department of Defense press statements (2012–2014); reported by The Christian Science Monitor, WIRED (Nov 2011, "Iran's GPS Spoofing").
R2 10 · Medium
Firmware signing key compromise → mass malicious update
Precedent ASUS Live Update supply-chain attack (2018, "ShadowHammer") — attackers compromised ASUS's build infrastructure and distributed a backdoored update to ~1M users via legitimate signing. Same single-key model.
Sector Consumer electronics — directly analogous to drone OTA
Source: Kaspersky Lab GReAT (Mar 2019); Motherboard (Mar 2019, "Inside ASUS Supply Chain Hack").
R3 16 · High
FlightHub admin credential theft → enterprise fleet data breach
Precedent Twilio breach (Aug 2022) — attackers phished employees via SMS (MFA fatigue / vishing), gained access to internal systems, exfiltrated customer data. Uber breach (Sep 2022) — same pattern: contractor phoned, social-engineered credentials.
Sector Cloud platforms; SaaS — identical to FlightHub exposure
Source: Twilio security bulletin (Aug 2022); Uber blog (Sep 2022, "Security Update").
R4 12 · High
Mobile SDK compromise in DJI Fly → user credential harvest
Precedent Samsung breach (Jul 2022) — attackers compromised a third-party SDK used by Samsung's internal services; accessed customer PII. XcodeGhost (2015) — trojanised Xcode SDK affected 100+ iOS apps in App Store.
Sector Mobile apps — same supply-chain structure
Source: BleepingComputer (Aug 2022, "Samsung confirms July data breach"); Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 (2015, "XcodeGhost").
R5 10 · Medium
Misconfigured cloud bucket → mass image / flight log leak
Precedent Toyota cloud misconfiguration (Oct 2022) exposed location data of 2.1M customers for a decade. Microsoft AI research team (Sep 2023) exposed 38TB of internal data via misconfigured SAS token.
Sector Cloud storage — same AWS/GCP/Azure risk profile
Source: Toyota press release (May 2023); Wiz Research blog (Sep 2023, "Microsoft AI Research").
R6 8 · Medium
Malicious insider exfiltrates autonomous flight AI models
Precedent Apple "Project Titan" data theft (Apr 2018) — former Apple engineer downloaded 2,400 files on autonomous vehicle project before joining Chinese EV startup XPeng. Pleaded guilty 2022.
Sector Autonomous systems — directly analogous tech domain
Source: US DOJ press release (May 2022, "Xiaolang Zhang Pleads Guilty").
R7 16 · High
Credential stuffing → mass DJI account takeover
Precedent Norton LifeLock breach (Jan 2023) — credential stuffing via leaked password databases compromised ~925k accounts. Activision Blizzard (Feb 2023) — credential stuffing exposed 30k+ accounts.
Sector Consumer accounts — same attack pattern
Source: NortonLifeLock data breach notification (Jan 2023); BleepingComputer (Feb 2023, "Activision breach").

Verizon DBIR 2024 reports credential abuse accounts for 77% of basic web application attacks (p. 18).

R8 5 · Low
Hardware implant in chip / sensor → silent telemetry exfiltration
Precedent Bloomberg Super Micro report (Oct 2018) — alleged Chinese subversion of server motherboards via tampered chips. Disputed by companies but shaped US policy. Huawei US Entity List restrictions (May 2019) — same trust framework.
Sector Supply chain — same exposure profile as drone components
Source: Bloomberg Businessweek (Oct 2018, "The Big Hack"); US Commerce Department Entity List filing (May 2019).

Stated assumption: Likelihood (1) reflects absence of any confirmed public case at DJI scale.

R9 15 · High
Geopolitical ban in major market → hundreds of millions in lost revenue
Precedent US Army memo (May 2017) ordered units to stop using DJI drones citing cybersecurity concerns. US Interior Dept grounding order (Jan 2020). US Countering CCP Drones Act (May 2024) — would ban DJI sales in US.
Sector Same organisation — already documented
Source: US Army memo (May 2017, via SUAS News); Congressional Record H4624 (May 2024).
R10 10 · Medium
Wireless protocol (OcuSync / Lightbridge) exploit → remote drone hijack
Precedent DEF CON drone villages (2016–2023) have repeatedly demonstrated RF hijacks of consumer drones. Parrot AR.Drone exploits (2014–2016) — public PoCs for command injection over WiFi.
Sector Wireless-controlled drones — same attack surface
Source: DEF CON archive (defcon.org); Rapid7 Metasploit module documentation (2015).

Stated assumption: Impact/likelihood depend on assumption that OcuSync contains unreported protocol flaws.

03

Peer-Incident Timeline (Drone & Adjacent)

Click any year to view context
2011 GPS Spoofing

Iran captures US RQ-170 Sentinel drone via GPS spoofing

Iranian forces spoof GPS coordinates to make the CIA drone believe it was landing at its Afghan home base; lands intact in Iranian territory. Confirmed by US officials (2012–2014).

2015 Supply Chain

XcodeGhost — trojanised iOS dev tool affects 100+ apps

Attackers poisoned a Chinese mirror of Xcode; hundreds of legitimate iOS apps compiled with it sent data back to attacker infrastructure. App Store accepted the apps.

2017 DJI Ban

US Army memo orders units to stop using DJI drones

US Army memo (May 2017) cited "known vulnerabilities" and "cyber security weaknesses" in DJI systems. Later expanded to the entire US Department of Defense and eventually other agencies.

2018 OTA / Build Pipeline

ASUS Live Update / ShadowHammer — ~1M devices backdoored

Attackers compromised ASUS's build pipeline; signed a malicious update with legitimate ASUS keys; pushed it to ~1M users. Direct precedent for R2.

2020 DJI Ban

US Department of Interior grounds all DJI drones

DOI ordered its ~800 drones grounded citing "security concerns". Pattern continues to expand through 2024.

2022 Phishing / Cloud

Twilio + Uber breaches via MFA-fatigue phishing

Both breaches in summer 2022 followed the same pattern: SMS-based MFA prompt bombing, employee accepts, attackers pivot internally. Direct precedent for R3.

2023 Misconfig

Microsoft AI research team leaks 38TB via misconfigured SAS token

Wiz Research discovered an exposed internal SAS token granting full access to training data, backups, and employee credentials. Direct precedent for R5.

2023 Credential Stuffing

Norton LifeLock: 925k accounts compromised via credential stuffing

Attackers used passwords leaked in other breaches to take over Norton LifeLock accounts. Direct precedent for R7.

2024 DJI Ban

US Countering CCP Drones Act introduced in Congress

Bill would prohibit DJI from operating in the US market if it remains on the US Entity List. Direct precedent for R9.

Why this matters for the assessment

Every numerical estimate in the Week 2 risk register and Week 3 ALE calculation can be traced to either a documented incident at a comparable organisation, an industry statistic from a named source, or an explicitly stated assumption (flagged in-line). No risk score or treatment recommendation is invented.

The two risks where no public data exists (R8 — hardware implant at DJI scale; R10 — specific OcuSync flaw) are marked with Likelihood = 1 and carry explicit "stated assumption" labels rather than fabricated precedent. This follows Week 3 Part 3 guidance: "where nothing is available, a clearly stated assumption."
