# DJI cyber risk governance case study

## Case purpose

This learning case connects the weekly practicals into one governance narrative. DJI is used as the coursework organisation for analysing safety-critical flight systems, customer and operational data, mobile and cloud services, firmware, wireless communications, and supply-chain dependencies.

## Core risk themes

| Theme | Example coursework scenario | Governance question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flight safety | Navigation manipulation or GPS anomaly affects drone behaviour. | Who owns the safety case, and what evidence validates the fail-safe response? |
| Identity and customer access | Credential stuffing enables unauthorised account access. | Are identity controls, logging, exception approvals, and recovery processes measurable and accountable? |
| Software and supply chain | A dependency or update path is compromised. | Are critical components inventoried, reviewed, and subject to release assurance? |
| Data protection | Media, telemetry, or flight information is exposed. | Are data minimisation, access control, encryption, and retention decisions demonstrable? |
| Market and regulatory resilience | Restrictions or other external change disrupt a material market or supplier. | Is the dependency mapped, owned, and supported by documented contingency options? |

## How the practicals develop the case

1. **Week 1** identifies assets and applies CIA/MSR thinking; integrity is central to navigation and flight control.
2. **Week 2** records explicit risk statements, ratings, owners, controls, and review triggers.
3. **Week 3** turns selected risks into transparent quantitative scenarios.
4. **Week 4** tests whether the data and figures support the conclusions drawn from those scenarios.
5. **Week 5** converts the analysis into objectives, KPIs, initiatives, and a staged roadmap.

## Evidence boundary

This is a coursework case study, not an assertion of DJI internal incidents, controls, or financial losses. Where the practicals use public context or synthetic data, they should be read as contextual evidence or modelling inputs, not as DJI-specific observations. The appropriate next step in a real organisation would be to validate assumptions using controlled access to incident, identity, telemetry, safety, support, supplier, and cost records.
